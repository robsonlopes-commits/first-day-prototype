# Science narratives and approved stats

Each item says where it is published. Use the wording style (supports, helps) and the asterisk or dagger conventions. If a number is not here, read the live page first.

## 1. Hidden hunger (Learn: `/pages/hidden-hunger`, `/pages/our-science`, blog `hidden-hunger-symptoms`, `hidden-hunger-in-kids`)
- A lack of nutrients, not a lack of food: a child can eat enough and still lack what their brain, immune system and growing body need.
- Over 2 billion people worldwide (UNICEF/National Library of Medicine), over 50% of children globally, 33 million Americans including 5 million children (blog).
- 80% of children do not eat enough fruits and vegetables (also "more than 80% of toddlers"). 93% of kids do not eat enough fruits or vegetables on the kids PDP infographic.
- It can quietly affect growth and development, mood and behavior, immune resilience, sleep quality, energy, slow healing, getting sick often.
- Causes in the blog: poor diet and processed foods, malabsorption, increased needs (growth), plus the food system below.
- Signs by nutrient (blog, general education): vitamin A (night vision, dry skin), D (bone aches, weak muscles, low mood), B12 (tingling, tiredness, memory), C (bleeding gums, frequent illness). Tell parents to talk to the pediatrician.

## 2. Vanishing nutrients (`/pages/vanishing-nutrients`, home "Our Food Is Not What It Used To Be")
- Fresh produce lost "up to 75%" (some pages 73%) of its nutrients over the last 50 years: yield over nutrient density, depleted soil, excess CO2 (up to 30% fewer nutrients in plants), long storage (loss of up to 77%).
- Example: you would need about 8 oranges today for the vitamin A of 1 orange 50 years ago.
- Families who eat plenty of produce can still be affected. Home page impact items: nutrients disappearing, etc.

## 3. Ultra-processed foods, magnesium and additives (`/pages/hidden-hunger`)
- About 70% of the average American diet is ultra-processed (NHLBI), calorie dense and nutrient poor, displacing minerals; magnesium is one of the most depleted (nervous system regulation, sleep quality, muscle function, stress response).
- Low magnesium intake has been associated with restlessness, irritability, trouble sleeping, fatigue, difficulty managing stress.
- Blog (women, sleep): stress, caffeine, alcohol and some medications deplete magnesium; magnesium helps regulate cortisol, supports GABA activity and muscle release, so the body can shift to rest and digest.
- Roughly two thirds of the calories kids and teens eat come from ultra-processed foods (teen brain article). 59% of foods contain additives (also on the Home/LP pages "Artificial Food Additives Are Found In 59% Of Foods Today").

## 4. Microplastics, heavy metals and pesticides (`/pages/hidden-hunger`, `/pages/heavy-metals-and-pesticides`)
- People consume up to 52 credit cards worth of microplastics a year (WWF), straining gut health and developing systems.
- Heavy metals (lead, arsenic, mercury, cadmium) enter food, water and soil through modern farming, industrial pollution, pipes and packaging; no federal standard for heavy metals in dietary supplements. "Federal standards only cover the bare minimum."
- Clean Label Project® certification tests 150+ contaminants (heavy metals, pesticides, plasticizers, glyphosate), unannounced finished product purchases. Toddlers, Kids, Teens, Women's and Men's multis, magnesium and tribiotics are certified (as of the page).

## 5. Over-supplementation (`/pages/vitamin-overconsumption`)
- Exceeding 100% Daily Value is unnecessary and can be harmful, especially fat soluble vitamins that build up. First Day never exceeds 100% DV and does not use cheap fillers. Frame as "supplements fill gaps, they do not overload the body", never as a medical warning to a specific person.

## 6. Bioavailability and forms (`/pages/our-science`)
- Folate: methylfolate (active form) instead of folic acid; up to 40% of people have the MTHFR variant that limits converting folic acid. Vitamin E natural not synthetic; vitamin B12 as methylcobalamin not cyanocobalamin; vitamin C works with folate to raise its absorption; vitamin A as beta-carotene.
- Kids LP: "Most brands push B6 past 100% DV"; "Most brands use folic acid"; "Most vitamins give a 4 year old a 17 year old dose" (the "Did You Know?" boxes on the new PDP).

## 7. Tribiotics (`what-are-tribiotics`, PDPs)
- Prebiotic (feeds good bacteria; Livaux golden kiwi), probiotic (Bacillus coagulans SNZ 1969), postbiotic (heat treated). Together they support the gut microbiome, immunity, oral health (Kids), skin health (Teens), feminine health and bloating (Women), athletic performance and recovery (Men), softer stools (Toddlers).

## 9. Teen brain (blog `the-science-behind-teenage-mood-swings-sleepiness-and-motivation-loss`, `your-teen-isnt-lazy-their-brain-might-just-be-running-low`)
- The prefrontal cortex (planning, impulse control, emotional regulation) matures only in the mid 20s while the amygdala runs the show; adolescent circadian rhythm shifts; the brain is one of the hungriest organs and rewires fast. Nutrients often low: D, B12, folate, magnesium, and choline (teen boys need about 550 mg a day, one egg has about 150 mg, about 90% of Americans fall short).
