# Copy and voice decisions

> **Superseded in part by the Sesame Workshop guidelines (2026-11-13, `sesame-street/references/license-guidelines.md`).** The character lines, stats and headings below were written before them and many break the rule that characters never talk about the product, name the brand, claim a benefit or give a CTA. Keep from this file the headings that play on the show and the voice of each character; rewrite every line a character says into a catchphrase or a generic true-to-character phrase.

All copy is subject to `firstday-copy` (and its owner feedback log, items 10 to 13 were added by this page). All new copy here is **invented and pending the owner's review**; the live words of every section stay as they are.

## Rules decided on this page
- **Marketing headings only** are rewritten. Transactional headings ("Delivery Frequency", "Quantity"), the product name (H1), the FAQ questions and the section texts are not.
- **Every marketing heading has a small play on the show.** Not always a name: a catchphrase or an element is enough ("Me Know A Helper!", "Rubber Duckie", "One, Two, Three", "Sunny Day", "Neighbor", "Count On", "Ah Ah Ah"). A plain heading is not accepted.
- **A character name only where it reads better**; in every heading it is forced ("nao precisa ter o nome em todos").
- Title case, no hyphens, dashes, semicolons or colons in new copy, no widows (use `text-wrap: balance` and check). **One exception: a colon after a named character who asks or wonders** ("Big Bird Wonders: What Is Going On In That Little Tummy?").
- **CTAs in title case**: "Order Today".
- Claims: only "support", "help", asterisks where the live page has them. No outcome promise in a character's mouth, no "loves the vitamins", no reward, the gummy is a supplement, never a treat. Cookie Monster says cookies are a sometimes food and tries new food, never eats the gummy. Do not make the 45 day guarantee a character's promise (Elmo does not say it).
- Do not quote lyrics; the wordplay on the theme song title is not used (needs approval). "Sunny day" and "neighbor" are plain words.
