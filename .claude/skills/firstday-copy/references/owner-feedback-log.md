# Owner copy feedback log (add new entries at the top, with the why)

Format: date, before, after, why. The pattern behind each correction is what matters.

## 2026-10 All-in-1 Bundle landing (Kids and Teens)
1. **Section title did not match its content.** The "Behind the Plate" block first listed credibility badges (Fights Hidden Hunger, Developed Alongside Harvard & Yale Scientists, Pediatrician-Approved, Clean Label Project® Purity Award) under "What Is Happening Behind The Plate". Owner: the title is not talking to the content. After: items that explain what is happening (food has fewer nutrients, hidden hunger builds quietly, the brain runs low on mood nutrients, brain signals need B vitamins, ultra-processed foods crowd out magnesium). Why: a title makes a promise, the body must keep it.
2. **Make it scientific and about causes.** Owner: "more scientific, explain what is happening in the world or in the body that causes these behaviors; all items in the same style and cohesive with the title." After: each item is a cause (world or body) with the behavior it produces, same kind of heading, same depth, facts from published First Day pages (75% nutrient loss, over 50% of children affected, B6 and serotonin, B vitamins and brain signaling, magnesium and ultra-processed foods). Why: mechanism is more persuasive and more emotional than claims, and a list must read as one set.
3. **A sibling broke the logic.** The bedtime card said "Bedtime Should Feel Calm" while the other three moments described what goes wrong. After: "Bedtime Turns Into A Battle". Why: siblings share perspective and tense; the ideal belongs to the solution, not to the problem run.
4. **Section title as a label.** "A Day With A Picky Eater / Four Moments, Four Ways To Help" became "If You Have A Picky Eater At Home, This Is Probably Your Routine" (and the Teens and other variants in the same voice). Why: recognition ("that is my routine") pulls the reader in; the cards then read as proof of the title.
5. **CTA label.** "Meet The Bundle" after the plate section became "Meet The Solution". Why: the section talked about the problem, so the button answers it instead of jumping to the product.
6. **Remove repeated or decorative lines.** Removed the "45-Day Money Back Guarantee" line under the plate button, the "Order by Oct 15th" note, the percent tiles and a highlighted gift line, the "A Note To Parents" empathy section, and the "40% off" note on the One-Time option. Why: each repeated something already said or decorated without persuading.
7. **Title accent and emphasis.** Titles use a plain hook plus an accent phrase in the brand color and bold; the first title of a run is larger. Why: the key phrase should be the one the reader remembers.
8. **Emoji only in chips.** Badges carry one emoji at the start ("☀️ Morning"); titles and body do not. Why: the production site uses emoji there only.
9. **Emphasize the price comparison for the bundle** and add the "Add Individually" option. Why: the offer logic (the bundle is cheaper than buying each alone) is the conversion argument and must be impossible to miss.
10. **Angle preference.** Owner example of a fresh angle worth leading with: growth for Teens. Why: prefer the angles being tested now over generic ones (see `firstday-whats-new`).
