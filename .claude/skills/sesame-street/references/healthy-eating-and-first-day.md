# Sesame's own work on food and health, and how it fits First Day

Sourced on 2026-11-09 (see `sources.md`). The point of this file: Sesame Workshop already has a clear, public voice about food. First Day pages must **sound like they belong to it** and must **never contradict it**.

## What Sesame Workshop has said and done about eating
- **Curriculum area "Eating Well"** sits inside "Healthy Minds and Bodies" next to emotional well-being, health and hygiene and social emotional skills (Sesame Workshop "About us").
- **Healthy Habits for Life** (launched about 2005, outreach from 2006): a nutrition and physical-activity initiative for young children and their child-care providers, with a Child Care Resource Kit, a DVD hosted by Elmo, a storybook, recipes and public service announcements with Elmo and Rosita. About 6 million families and child care centers received the toolkit. A Penn State clearinghouse found **no peer-reviewed publications** evaluating its effectiveness: so do not cite it as proof of outcomes.
- **"Cookies are a sometimes food."** In 2005 the Workshop reframed cookies from "an anytime food" to "a **sometimes** food" and Cookie Monster has repeated it (2007 on Martha Stewart, 2008 on The Colbert Report). The Workshop said the goal was **moderation, not prohibition**. The change drew criticism and a petition of more than 3,000 signatures, and rumors that he would become "Veggie Monster" were denied. A 2025 Sesame Workshop printable still says Cookie Monster loves cookies but tries healthy food too, and shows **Anytime Foods** (fruits, vegetables, whole grains, lean meat, low-fat dairy) versus **Sometimes Foods** (cookies, cake, candy, chips), plus a prompt to watch Cookie Monster's little niece try a new food.
- **Cookie Monster's Foodie Truck** (2018 on): Cookie Monster and Gonger fill orders from children, teaching where food comes from and healthier habits. Returned in the 2025 Netflix season. **Cookie Monster's Cookie Cart** (2025) is a baking and mishap segment, not a health segment.
- **Eat Brighter!** (with the Produce Marketing Association; the sources found are progress reports from 2016 and 2017, the launch year and current status were not confirmed): Sesame characters on fresh produce, royalty-free licensing for growers and retailers, aimed at children 2 to 5 and their parents. Sesame Workshop's own (internal, not peer reviewed) 2007 research reported that a character on broccoli moved children's choices from 22% to 50% broccoli; partner surveys reported 3 to 5% sales increases. Treat these as **industry-reported, not independent science**, and never print them as claims on a First Day page.
- **Elmo and healthy eating:** Elmo appeared in a Food Network healthy-eating special (2005) and joined Michelle Obama in 2013 to promote fresh fruits and vegetables (Wikipedia).
- **Research that matters for tone:** the Workshop and others report that children relate to characters emotionally and that a familiar character makes children more willing to try foods. This is why character-led nutrition works for picky eating; it is also why the guardrails below matter (critics note it uses the tools of junk-food marketing).

## The fit with First Day
| First Day idea | Sesame way in | Keep the line |
|---|---|---|
| Picky eating, "the plate pushed away" | Cookie Monster tries a new food; Elmo curiosity; Oscar's grouchy no | Normalize the refusal, show a small step, never pressure |
| Hidden hunger, the 21 organic superfoods | Abby discovers what is hiding; the Count counts nutrients | Real facts only; no diagnosis |
| Sleep and bedtime (magnesium) | Telly worries, Bert and Ernie bedtime, calm-down "breath" | No medical promise, melatonin-free stays the product fact |
| Focus and learning | The Count, Grover, Big Bird learning | Supports, never cures |
| Gut and tummy | Cookie Monster's tummy, Foodie Truck "what goes in" | Supports digestion*, keep it playful, not clinical |
| One routine for the whole family | Sesame Street itself: different neighbors, one street | Each person gets the formula for their age |

## Guardrails (read before any food, gummy or reward story)
1. **A gummy is a supplement, not candy.** Sesame's own framework puts candy under "sometimes foods". Never call the product a treat, candy or "yummy cookie"; never have Cookie Monster eat vitamins as a snack. Show the **grown-up giving the dose** as directed (2 gummies a day on the multi). Taste can be mentioned plainly (flavors are real), as the live pages already do.
2. **Never use the character to push a child to take more, faster or "to be good".** No rewards ("if you eat your broccoli, you get a gummy"). No guilt.
3. **Anytime and sometimes** is Sesame's vocabulary. Do not invent "good food and bad food". Do not put First Day in the "anytime foods" column (it is not a food).
4. **No health claim in a character's mouth beyond First Day's approved claims** (see `firstday-brand-knowledge/references/science-and-claims.md`): "supports", asterisks, daggers for survey numbers.
5. **No weight, body or growth shaming.** First Day's growth angle is the parent's worry, never the child's body (see the owner-feedback log). With characters aged 3 to 8, this matters even more.
6. **Competitor in the same license:** Sesame Street vitamin gummies already exist (Webber Naturals, sold in Canada: multivitamin, vitamin C, vitamin D3, "Incredible Power Multi"). Do not copy their packaging or wording, and do not assume a US exclusivity. The owner knows the exact partnership terms: ask before any category claim like "the only" or "first".
7. **No false partnership signals** until approved: no "official", "approved", "endorsed by Sesame Workshop" or a Workshop logo lockup unless the owner confirms (see `brand-rules.md`).
8. **Kids' data and ads:** a children's character page is child-directed content. Do not ask for a child's personal data in a quiz or form; address the parent. (If the exploration collects any data, flag it to the owner for review, children's privacy law is not covered here.)
