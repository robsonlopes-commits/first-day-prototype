# Brand, art and licensing rules

What this file knows comes from the owner's art library (the "House Art Style" files received on 2026-11-09) and public sources. **The partnership agreement itself was not seen.** Where this file says "ask", the owner or Sesame Workshop decides.

## What the owner's art library says about itself
- The library is Sesame Workshop's **"House Style" Consumer Products Style Guide** (a cover page dated 2026, copyright line "Sesame Street® and associated characters, trademarks and design elements are owned and licensed by Sesame Workshop. © 2026 Sesame Workshop. All rights reserved."). The cover shows Big Bird, Abby Cadabby, Grover, Elmo, Cookie Monster and the puppy **Tango** on a park background, and the **SESAME STREET sign logo** (the green "123" sign with the yellow border).
- **Three versions of every piece of art: A (full color), B (limited color), C (outline).** The sheets say **"DO NOT mix Versions A, B, and C together"** and **"how art is used within the context of specific applications must be approved on a case-by-case basis"**. Pick one version per layout.
- **Small print on some sheets mentions scaling** (characters shown reduced to fit the template). It was not read in detail: open the sheet and read it before resizing any character group.
- **"Specification Chart A / Full Shading Version A"**: a character roster with the color specification (Pantone references and CMYK for "Sesame Black") of Big Bird, Cookie Monster, Abby Cadabby, Elmo, Zoe, Grover and Super Grover, Ernie, Bert, Prairie Dawn, Oscar the Grouch, Baby Bear, Telly Monster, Rosita, Mr. Snuffleupagus and the Count. A footnote says the colors shown on screen are not intended to match the Pantone color standards. Use that chart (not a screenshot) when a color must be exact.
- **Some art has crop guides** ("crop" areas marked on legs and bodies: a character is meant to be cropped there, for example in a banner). Respect them.
- **File naming in the library** (codes seen: HS and HS2 = House Style and House Style 2; EL, CM, GR, GRP, OG, TG, CH, AC, CT, ZO, ICN, BKG = backgrounds, PTN = patterns, PROPS, STEM Lab and STEM Nature = a STEM supplement; "Tales from 123" CP guide). Only BKG, PTN, PROPS, ICN and STEM are clear from the names, the rest is a guess: open the thumbnail before assuming what a file is.
- Some files in the library are **layered** (hidden layers, "unlock layers according to application requirements"). The SVG conversion flattens them: one SVG shows the whole artboard with all versions and the instruction text. Extracting a single version or element is manual work, done only for the pieces that will be used.

## Using the art
0. **Owner decisions (2026-11-10): use Version B (limited color) of every piece.** The owner also allowed the image generation API, but only with the SVGs of the library as references (and the official logo SVG and the Sesame Street bottle that the owner provided). **Picture slots rule (2026-11-10): on a page that becomes a Shopify template, a picture or video slot is an image field. It holds a file (the live image, or an image generated with the API from the live image and the Sesame bottle as references). SVGs only sit on top of it as stickers, or live in new sections and HTML/CSS changes. Never replace a photo with a montage of SVGs built in HTML.** Characters are never generated from words: when an image is generated, the characters come from the SVG stickers on top of it.
1. **Only from the library**, never redrawn, traced, recolored, generated by an image model or recreated from a screenshot. The image-generation skill must not generate Sesame characters.
2. **One version per layout** (A, B or C). No mixing.
3. **Logo:** the Sesame Street sign and the Sesame Workshop logo have their own usage rules that this library does not include. **Ask the owner** before placing either, and before any "Sesame Street x First Day" lockup.
4. **Approval is per use.** The sheets say so. Treat every page as "pending approval by Sesame Workshop" until the owner confirms.
5. **Kermit the Frog is not owned by Sesame Workshop** (Wikipedia); he is not part of the House Style roster. Never use him.
6. **Do not commit licensed art to the public GitHub repository** (`robsonlopes-commits/first-day-prototype` is public and deployed on Vercel) unless the owner confirms the license and approval cover publishing it. For private work keep the art outside the repo, or in a private location, and wire it in only after the owner approves. A prototype that is shared with the partner is a different matter: ask.
7. The converted SVG library lives outside the project (`~/Downloads/House Art Style - SVG/`: `svg/` the artboards, `previews/` thumbnails, `index.html`, `inventory.csv`; the official logo is `svg/USA_CMYK_main.svg`). Move only the pieces used, optimized, after approval.
8. **Taking one piece out of an artboard:** every artboard holds Versions A, B and C side by side (the middle panel is B) plus the instruction text, and the converted files are flat. `tools/svg-crop.py in.svg out.svg x y w h` keeps only the paths and images inside a box (in the SVG's own units), so a Version B figure becomes a standalone SVG of 20 to 200 KB (instead of 2 to 80 MB). The box of the B panel is found by rendering the artboard to PNG, locating the green "Version B" bar and the non-white pixels above it (see the scripts used on 2026-11-10 in the exploration notes). Check every crop by eye: some poses come with a second figure, a floating head, a prop or a caption (Big Bird's note) and need a tighter box. The cropped pieces used so far are in `public/assets/sesame/` (characters, props, logo, the owner's bottle). **Untracked until the owner approves publishing them.**

## Words to avoid until approved
"Official partner", "approved by", "endorsed by", "recommended by Sesame Street", "Sesame Workshop research shows", any outcome promise tied to a character, any statement that a character "takes" or "loves" the vitamins, and any Sesame Workshop statistic. Facts about the show's history and mission are public and can be stated plainly if needed, with the source in the page notes.

## Open questions for the owner (ask when a page needs them)
- Which pages and audiences does the license cover (kids only, or also teens, women, men)?
- Are the characters allowed to speak copy (dialogue), or only appear as art?
- May the Sesame Street logo and the Workshop logo be used, and where?
- Is parody (format 12) or title wordplay (format 13) allowed?
- What does the approval process look like (who, how long, what to send)?
- Are there product claims or words the partner forbids (candy, treat, reward)?
- May the partnership be mentioned publicly yet, and when?
