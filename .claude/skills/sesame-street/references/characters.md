# The characters

Everything here was read from Wikipedia, Sesame Workshop's own character page and press releases on 2026-11-09 (see `sources.md`). "Voice" means how the character talks and sounds in writing. **Performers** are listed because the partnership may need credits and because the names change over time; check before printing any. A catchphrase marked (fan list) comes from a fan-voted list, not an official one: verify before using it in public copy.

## The core cast

**Elmo** (red monster, "3½ years old", birthday February 3)
- Want: to know, to play, to be kind. "Enthusiastic, friendly, curious" (Muppet list); performer Kevin Clash said Elmo should be "kind and loving".
- Voice: high falsetto, **talks about himself in the third person** ("Elmo"), a **contagious giggle**. Short, bouncy, full of questions. "Elmo loves you!" is his best known line (fan list). Toddler logic, never sarcastic.
- Bits: *Elmo's World* (hosts, with Mr. Noodle and Dorothy the goldfish), "Elmo's Pretend" (2025), Tango his puppy (Louie is his dad, Mae his mom), Smartie the yellow phone sidekick ("Look it up", from 2017). Best friends: Zoe and Abby. Season 57: uses a **heart breath** to calm down.
- Performers: Brian Muehl (1980 to 1984), Richard Hunt (1984 to 1985), Kevin Clash (1985 to 2012), Ryan Dillon (2013 to present).
- Good for: the child's own point of view, curiosity, big feelings, trying new things, the "playdate with the reader" tone.

**Cookie Monster** (blue monster, real name Sid)
- Want: cookies, and now also to try other foods ("Cookies are a sometimes food", since 2005).
- Voice: refers to himself as **"Me"**, drops grammar ("Me want cookie!"), loud eating sounds (fan list: the "om nom nom" noise). Big heart, frenzied but cuddly.
- Bits: **"C Is for Cookie"** (first aired 1971 to 1972; title only, never quote lyrics), **Monsterpiece Theater** (as "Alistair Cookie", a parody of Masterpiece Theatre), **Cookie Monster's Foodie Truck** (with Gonger, 2018) and the Season 56 **Cookie Cart**. He eats fruits and vegetables too and "tries healthy food".
- Performers: Frank Oz (1969 to 2004), David Rudman (2001 to present).
- Good for: food, picky eating, "sometimes and anytime foods", hunger jokes, counting snacks with the Count. **Handle with care around gummies** (see `healthy-eating-and-first-day.md`).

**Big Bird** (yellow bird, 8'2", "psychologically about six years old")
- Want: to learn and to be a good friend. Gentle, childlike, innocent; learns by getting things wrong.
- Voice: simple, sincere, slow, sometimes **mispronounces names** (calls Mr. Hooper "Mr. Looper"), sings in a plain "la la" style (fan list).
- Bits: his nest behind 123, his teddy bear Radar, best friend **Mr. Snuffleupagus**, "Follow That Bird" (1985 film). The grown-ups finally believed him about Snuffy in 1985.
- Performers: Caroll Spinney (1969 to 2018), Matt Vogel (understudy from 1996, main since 2018).
- Good for: growth, "why" questions, honesty, being believed, being new at something.

**Grover** (blue monster)
- Want: to help (Super Grover) or to serve (waiter Grover). "An infinitely optimistic soul", timid and self-conscious, accident-prone.
- Voice: earnest, **rarely uses contractions** ("I am", "do not"), excited, a little over-formal. "And I am cute, too!" (Super Grover). Super Grover 2.0 tagline "He Shows Up!" (2010).
- Bits: **Super Grover** (cape and helmet, crash landings), **waiter Grover with Mr. Johnson** (customer sketches that taught same and different), **Mail Monster** (Season 57), **Grover's Suity Suitcase** (2025).
- Performers: Frank Oz (1970 to 2012), Eric Jacobson (1998 to present).
- Good for: "to the rescue" stories, helping a friend, service, delivery and subscription jokes (Mail Monster), earnest instructions.

**Oscar the Grouch** (green grouch)
- Want: to be left alone with his trash. Dislikes anything "nice" (rainbows, candy, chocolate on Sesame Workshop's page); gives children permission to feel grouchy; softens for young children.
- Voice: gruff, dismissive, "Scram!" (fan list), "I love trash" is a song title. His grumpiness is a joke about **opposites**, never meanness toward a child.
- Bits: the trash can that is bigger inside (Slimey the worm, Fluffy the elephant, Grouchland gate), girlfriend Grundgetta, "Grouchketeers", superintendent of 123 in Tales from 123 (2025).
- Performers: Caroll Spinney (1969 to 2018), Eric Jacobson (understudy from 2015, main since 2018).
- Good for: the "no, thanks" voice (a picky plate told as a Grouch joke), cleaning, the "what is hiding in there" reveal, honest grumpiness about feelings.

**Bert and Ernie** (roommates)
- Dynamic: Ernie is naive, exuberant, playful and mischievous; Bert is serious, neat, "world-weary", with dull-seeming tastes (paper clips, bottle caps, oatmeal, pigeons). Typical sketch: Ernie has a far-fetched idea, Bert tries to talk him out of it and loses his temper, Ernie stays oblivious. Ernie sometimes eats Bert's snack and covers it up.
- Bits: Ernie's **"Rubber Duckie"** (number 16 on the Billboard Hot 100 in September 1970), Bert's **"Doin' the Pigeon"**, Caveman Days (1971 to 1990), "Christmas Eve on Sesame Street" (1978). Sesame Workshop has said the puppets have no sexual orientation.
- Performers: Bert: Frank Oz (1969 to 2006), Eric Jacobson (since 1998). Ernie: Jim Henson (1969 to 1990), Steve Whitmire, Billy Barkhurst, Peter Linz (2017 to present).
- Good for: two-voice dialogues (the kid who wants everything versus the parent who plans), bedtime and bath scenes, "same and different".

**The Count (Count von Count)** (vampire, debuted 1972)
- Want: to count everything; sad when there is nothing to count; loses his temper when interrupted.
- Voice: **"Greetings! I am the Count"**, a staccato laugh **"Ah! Ah! Ah!"** after each number, thunder and lightning when he reaches the total, the Countmobile, a squeaky castle door, girlfriends such as Countess Dahling von Dahling. Friendly and cheerful (he was villainous in the first seasons, toned down in 1975).
- Performers: Jerry Nelson (1972 to 2012), Matt Vogel (2013 to present).
- Good for: **numbers that matter** (2 gummies a day, 30 servings, 21 superfoods, 4 bottles, 45% off): the Count is the natural way to make a stat memorable.

**Abby Cadabby** (pink fairy-in-training, 4)
- Want: to learn magic and make friends. Her magic is limited (pops in and out, floats when happy, turns things into pumpkins), and she is amazed by ordinary skills like counting or drawing letters.
- Voice: bright, sparkly, **"That's so magic!"**, "gotta poof". Elmo is her best friend; Rudy is her stepbrother; her mother is the Fairy Godmother. Debuted August 14, 2006.
- Bits: Abby's Flying Fairy School, **Abby's Magic Beasties** (2025: Bunberry, musical dragon triplets, Bubble Bug), the Beastie Bestie episode (Season 57, sadness and memory boxes).
- Performer: Leslie Carrara-Rudolph.
- Good for: invisible-made-visible stories ("what is hiding behind the plate"), wonder, trying something that goes a little wrong, memory and feelings.

## Everyone else (the roster of the Specification Chart and the character page)
- **Rosita**: turquoise monster, 5, from Mexico, first regular **bilingual** Muppet (English and Spanish), plays guitar, introduced 1991; full name Rosita la Monstrua de las Cuevas. Performer Carmen Osbahr.
- **Zoe**: orange monster, 3, loves to sing and dance, best friend of Elmo, pet rock Rocco. Performer Jennifer Barnhart (since 2016).
- **Telly Monster**: "intense and earnest monster who worries over everything", loves triangles, best friend Baby Bear. A natural voice for worried parents and nighttime worries. Performers Brian Muehl and Martin P. Robinson.
- **Baby Bear**: adapted from The Three Bears; adventurous and artistic. (Mama Bear exists; no debut date found.)
- **Mr. Snuffleupagus (Snuffy)**: big, brown, anteater-like, Big Bird's best friend; for years only Big Bird saw him ("imaginary friend") until the 1985 reveal. Performer Martin P. Robinson (since 1981). Story value: **believing the child**.
- **Julia**: a 4-year-old with autism, debuted in books (2015) and on TV (2017). Performer Stacey Gordon. **Handle with the greatest care**: never use her as a prop for a product story.
- **Gabrielle** (6¾), **Tamir** (8, her cousin), **Ji-Young** (7, electric guitar, the show's first Asian American Muppet, 2021), **Wes** (5) and **Elijah** (a 35-year-old meteorologist, 2021), **Rudy** (3, orange fur and blue curls, Abby's stepbrother, TV debut 2017), **Nina** (on the street since 2016), **Tango** (Elmo's puppy, from a 2021 special), **Gonger** (little pink monster, Cookie's food truck partner), **Smartie** (Elmo's phone), **Murray Monster** (host of "Word on the Street", with Ovejita the lamb), **Prairie Dawn** (precocious, "psychological age of seven"), **Herry Monster** (blue, big and burly), **Forgetful Jones**, **Natasha** (baby monster), **Kermit the Frog** (appeared from the first season, **not owned by Sesame Workshop**: never use Kermit without separate rights).
- Humans such as Gordon, Susan, Maria, Luis, Bob, Linda, Mr. Hooper, Mr. Noodle and Mr. Johnson (Grover's customer) exist. Their details were not verified: do not feature a human character without checking.

## Casting table (which voice for which job)
| Job on a First Day page | Best lead | Why (from canon) | Watch out |
|---|---|---|---|
| The plate pushed away, trying a new food | **Cookie Monster** (Foodie Truck) or **Elmo** | Cookie tries healthy food too; Elmo is curious and kind | Do not make food a reward or a fight. "Sometimes food" is the Sesame message |
| The picky "no, thank you" | **Oscar** | He dislikes nice things: the grouchy no as a joke | Never mock a real child's refusal |
| Numbers, facts, stats | **The Count** | He counts everything and celebrates the total | Keep the number real and sourced |
| Rescue, "we have a solution" | **Grover / Super Grover**, Mail Monster for delivery | Earnest helper who shows up | Grover is clumsy: let him succeed in the end |
| Wonder and the invisible (hidden hunger, what is happening in the body) | **Abby Cadabby** | Magic, "That's so magic!", wonder at ordinary things | No magic cure claims: the magic is the discovery, not the product |
| Worry, bedtime nerves, the parent's side | **Telly Monster** | Earnest worrier | Do not scare |
| Growth, learning, being believed | **Big Bird** (with Snuffy) | Child-like questions, honesty | Keep him simple |
| Two people with opposite plans (child versus parent, routine versus chaos) | **Bert and Ernie** | The odd couple dynamic | No romantic framing |
| Big feelings, kindness | **Elmo, Abby, Zoe, Gabrielle, Rudy** | Season 56 to 57 curriculum | Name the feeling, show a calming step |
| Music, rhythm, a jingle | **Zoe, Rosita, Ji-Young, Ernie** | Sing and dance, guitar | Never reuse lyrics of existing songs; titles only |
| Spanish and bilingual moments | **Rosita** | First regular bilingual Muppet | Use correct Spanish, ask a native reviewer |

Teens, women and men: the show is built for children about 2 to 5 (the characters are 3 to 8 years old). For older audiences the natural entry is **nostalgia and the parent wink**, not the character talking to a teen. Ask the owner how far the partnership allows before writing character copy for teens or adults.
