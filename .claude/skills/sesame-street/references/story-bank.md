# Story bank (a brainstorm, not canon, not approved)

Every idea here is **invented for this guide** from the real formats and traits in the other files. None is an episode, a script or something Sesame Workshop approved. The partnership terms decide what is allowed: **the owner explains the pages and the approvals**. Use this bank to start thinking, then check each idea against `characters.md`, `voice-and-humor.md`, `healthy-eating-and-first-day.md` and `brand-rules.md`.

## The five-beat mini story (works for a hero, a section or an email)
1. **The moment** (First Day voice, real): the plate pushed away, the 3 pm crash, the one more story.
2. **The character arrives with a want** (one lead, one helper at most).
3. **A gentle mix-up** (a funny mistake, never a failure of the child or the parent).
4. **The reveal** (one real fact or mechanism, in plain First Day text with its asterisk if it needs one).
5. **The tiny step** (the action: build the box, pick the product), said by the character in their voice and repeated by the plain button.

## Ideas by format of the show

**1. A Foodie Truck order for each person** (Family Box Builder, quiz steps)
The page is a food truck that takes **orders**: "Who is ordering today?" A character runs the window (Gonger and Elmo are safer than Cookie Monster near a supplement, see the guardrails). Each family member gets an order ticket, the summary is the order, the free gifts are what the truck hands out with it. Copy tone: friendly service banter, every line one short sentence.

**2. Super Grover and the Four Missions** (four hero products: nutrition, gut, focus, sleep)
Grover lands, gets it slightly wrong, then completes **four short missions** (one per product). Mail Monster Grover is the delivery and subscription voice ("Your box is on its way"). Fits the All-in-1 and the bundle pages.

**3. The Count counts it** (stats, timelines, pricing tiers)
Any number that matters is announced by the Count: 2 gummies, 30 days, 21 superfoods, 4 bottles, 45% off, the 7 and 30 and 60 day timeline. Thunder and lightning when the total arrives (the fourth bottle, the fourth free gift). The Count is the safest character for stat sections: the number must be real.

**4. What is in Oscar's can?** (FAQ, "what is behind the plate")
Oscar opens his trash can and pulls out causes one by one, each a short, true line (the "Behind the Plate" style accordion). First Day's **No Junk** promise lands as a joke: Oscar looks for junk in the gummy and cannot find any. Never imply Oscar endorses; the joke is the contrast.

**5. Abby's Magic Beasties** (hidden hunger, "what is happening in the body")
The invisible is made visible: Abby looks for a Beastie that cannot be seen (hidden hunger: a lack of nutrients, not a lack of food). Wonder, never fear. The magic is the discovery, the fix is a real, plain First Day line with its asterisk.

**6. Telly's Worry List** (parent FAQ)
Telly worries aloud (is it too green? will they sleep? is it too late to start?) and a friend answers calmly. Gives the parent's real questions a warm, funny voice.

**7. Bert and Ernie at bedtime** (Nighttime Reset Magnesium)
Ernie wants one more everything, Bert wants lights out. They end up calm. The plain product text states what is real (calming ingredients, no melatonin hormones, supports restful sleep*). No cure, no promise.

**8. Big Bird asks why** (education blocks, "How This Helps")
Big Bird asks the plain question; a friend gives the answer; the section repeats the pattern for each nutrient. Fits the science tabs by person.

**9. Elmo's question** (quizzes, "Who are you shopping for?")
A quiz where Elmo asks the questions to the child's grown-up in his own third-person voice ("Elmo wants to know..."), one question per screen, play-along style. The recap says what was added and why, in a friendly sentence.

**10. 123 Sesame Street, the building** (family navigation)
After Tales from 123 opened the building, each **door or window** can be a person in the family (Kid, Toddler, Teen, Mom, Dad). "Knock on a door to build their box." The stoop is the page start, the elevator or stairs are steps. Fits the Family Box Builder and any "pick a person" screen.

**11. Mail Monster Grover delivers** (shipping, free gifts, subscription)
"A delivery for the whole family." Free gifts are packages Grover tries to deliver (and fumbles, then succeeds). Keep real facts (free shipping on the first order, 45 day guarantee) in plain text.

**12. Monsterpiece Theater parody** (the long-form "why us" page)
"Alistair Cookie presents" a mock-prestige drama about the vitamin. The adult layer is the parody of prestige TV, the child layer is the cookie. Only if the partnership allows parody of the characters and **never with claims inside the sketch**.

**13. "Can you tell me how to get to..." wordplay** (headlines)
The show's theme-song title is a famous phrase. Wordplay on the **title** ("how to get to dinner without a fight") is fun but may be treated as a reference to a licensed work: ask first. Never use or paraphrase the lyrics.

**14. Zoe and Rosita sing and dance** (energy, routine)
Short rhythmic lines for a morning routine block, with Rosita giving a bilingual moment (reviewed by a native speaker). Titles only for music references.

## Mini examples in the voice (invented, for tone only)

Button: See What Helps

**The Count, a stat block**
Title: The Count Is Counting Your Day
Body: One bottle! Two gummies a day! Thirty days in a bottle! Ah ah ah! Some numbers are worth counting.
(then the real stat, with its source and asterisk, in plain First Day text)

**Oscar, an FAQ**
Question: Is There Any Junk In There?
Answer: Oscar looked everywhere. No fake sugars. No artificial colors. No fillers. Oscar is very disappointed. (plain line below: "Our gummies have no fake sugars, no artificial colors and no fillers.")

Check each example against the checks in `SKILL.md` before reusing a pattern: the examples are only for rhythm.

## Page types and where Sesame fits (ask the owner for the real plan)
- **Hero and headline:** one lead character, one sentence, the real moment first.
- **Product pages (PDP):** keep the existing PDP structure; characters belong in banners, badges, a "meet the family" strip, packaging shots and the gallery, not in the buy box math.
- **Landing pages:** story sections (formats 3, 4, 5, 6, 8) between real-fact sections.
- **Bundles and builders:** formats 1, 2, 10, 11.
- **Quizzes:** format 9.
- **Emails, ads, inserts:** formats 3, 11, 14 with the shortest lines.
- **Packaging and art:** only licensed art from the owner's library (see `brand-rules.md`).
