# Sesame Street: the world, the history, the format

Facts below come from the sources listed in `sources.md` (checked 2026-11-09). Where the sources disagree or only one weak source exists, it says so.

## What it is
- A children's educational TV series, premiered **November 10, 1969** on US public television (first network credit NET; PBS from November 9, 1970). Created by **Joan Ganz Cooney and Lloyd Morrisett**; produced by **Sesame Workshop**, called the Children's Television Workshop (CTW) until June 2000. The name comes from "open sesame".
- **Funding at launch:** about US$8 million from the Carnegie Foundation, the Ford Foundation, the Corporation for Public Broadcasting and the US government. **Jim Henson** joined the 1968 planning seminars and then the Muppets became the heart of the show.
- **First show to use educational goals and a curriculum**, and the first whose effects were formally studied (ETS studies in 1970 and 1971, over 1,000 studies by 2001). The **CTW Model** is the loop of planning, production and evaluation with producers, writers, educators and researchers working together. Original goal: prepare young children for school, especially from low-income families; cognitive goals plus affective goals (self-esteem, tolerance, conflict resolution).
- **The fantasy and reality finding:** early testing showed children lost interest in the human "street" scenes, so Big Bird and Oscar were made to interact with the humans and the Muppets were mixed in. First Day lesson: a real moment plus a character beats either alone.
- **Reach:** by 1979 about nine million American children under six watched daily; by 2019, 190 million children had watched over 160 versions in 70 languages (the show's own international co-productions began soon after the US debut). Over 200 Emmys (counts differ by year: 205 in 2021, 222 in 2022 per Wikipedia) and 11 Grammys.
- **Mission today** (Sesame Workshop "About us"): help children "grow smarter, stronger, and kinder"; reaches children in 190 countries; curriculum areas are **Early Learning** (STEM, creative arts, financial education, language and literacy, math, school readiness) and **Healthy Minds and Bodies** (emotional well-being, **eating well**, health and hygiene, social emotional skills). 74% of parents say Sesame Street prepares their child for school (2025 Sesame Brand Tracking Study, the Workshop's own number).
- **Executive producer:** Benjamin Lehmann (2018 to present, per Wikipedia). Recorded at Kaufman Astoria Studios (since 1993).

## The place
- **123 Sesame Street**: a brownstone apartment building on a city street, with a stoop. Big Bird's nest is behind the building next to Oscar's trash can; Oscar lives in the can (it is bigger inside: Slimey the worm, Fluffy the elephant, a gate to Grouchland). Since Season 56 the show also goes **inside** the building (see below).
- Other places named in the sources: Hooper's Store, the Fix-It Shop (where Maria fixed Abby's wand on her debut). Humans on the street have included Gordon, Susan, Maria, Luis, Bob, Linda, Mr. Hooper, Gina and others (the roster of humans is not covered in detail here: ask or check before naming one).
- **Mr. Noodle** (Elmo's World) and **Dorothy** the goldfish are recurring Elmo's World figures (Dorothy is widely known but was not in the pages fetched; treat as high confidence, verify before featuring).

## Format, then and now
- **Mix of styles:** live action street scenes, puppet sketches, animation, short films, songs, celebrity guests, parodies.
- **Magazine format** for decades (a 60 minute hour of short segments). By Season 20, research showed children could follow a story, so the street scenes became storylines; the format became more narrative in 2002.
- **Elmo's World** (premiered November 16, 1998): 15 minutes hosted by Elmo, toddler-centered topics (balls, dancing), heavy repetition, Mr. Noodle and Dorothy do not speak so Elmo does all the talking; "a playdate between the child and Elmo". Cut to about 5 minutes from 2017.
- **Half-hour** version from 2014 (Season 45), full hour retired in 2017.
- **Cookie Monster's Foodie Truck:** a recurring healthy-food segment (about 5 minutes, introduced 2018, Season 48): Cookie Monster and Gonger run a food truck and fill orders from children by video message; it teaches where food comes from and promotes healthier habits.

## Where it airs now (as of 2026-11-09)
- **Season 56** premiered **November 10, 2025** (Sesame Street Day, the 56th anniversary) on **Netflix and PBS KIDS on the same day** (Netflix deal announced May 19, 2025, including about 90 hours of older episodes; Netflix streams in 30+ languages; PBS stays free in the US). HBO (2016 to 2020) and HBO Max (2020 to 2025) are over for new episodes; older episodes stay in the HBO Max library until 2027 and the official YouTube channel started hosting a large archive of older episodes in January 2026.
- **Season 57** premieres **Monday, November 23, 2026** on Netflix and PBS KIDS (Sesame Workshop press release of October 23, 2026). Theme: **kindness, compassion and big feelings** (sharing, listening, other points of view, solving conflicts, apologizing). Episodes named in the release: 5701 Elmo & Tango's Puppy Playdate, 5702 Elmo's Treasure Hunt, 5703 Abby's Beastie Bestie, 5704 Elmo's Game Night; guests Zara Larsson, Post Malone, Tyler James Williams, Michelle Buteau; Mail Monster Grover and Super Grover appear; Elmo uses a "heart breath" to calm his jealousy.

## The reimagined format since Season 56 (the one parents see today)
- Longer **11-minute narrative stories** (no more magazine segments), built to be **played along** with, characters **speak to camera** to the child, and animated overlays add sparkles, bubbles and confetti.
- **New neighborhood access:** Season 56 shows the inside of 123 Sesame Street for the first time through **Tales from 123**, a five minute animated segment hosted by **Elmo and Tango**; residents include Cookie Monster, Big Bird, Bert, Ernie, Grover, Abby and **Oscar as the building superintendent**; gags and places named: a loud megaphone, a zany elevator, musical stairs. Early episode titles seen: "Finding Grover", "Peace and Quiet", "Elmo Helps Cookie Open His Door" (from a fan database, verify before using).
- **New segments:** *Elmo's Pretend* (Elmo's imaginative storytelling), *Cookie Monster's Cookie Cart* (Cookie Monster's new cart with experiments and silly daily specials), *Grover's Suity Suitcase* (Grover turns into different characters to help friends), *Abby's Magic Beasties* (Abby cares for magical creatures such as Bunberry, musical dragon triplets and Bubble Bug); *Elmo's World* and *Cookie Monster's Foodie Truck* return.
- **Curriculum:** emotional well-being, kindness, social problem solving (taking turns, working together, naming and managing big feelings, calming strategies).
- Other products: *Sesame Street: Neighborhood Adventures* on Roblox (immersive digital experience), the Webber Naturals Sesame Street vitamin gummies sold in Canada (a licensed competitor product, see `healthy-eating-and-first-day.md`).

## A short timeline for story hooks
- 1969: premiere; Big Bird, Oscar, Bert and Ernie, Kermit and Cookie Monster in the first episode.
- 1970: "Rubber Duckie" (Ernie) reaches number 16 on the Billboard Hot 100; Grover gets his name.
- 1971 and 1972: "C Is for Cookie" first airs; Mr. Snuffleupagus appears (November 15, 1971); the Count debuts (1972).
- 1979 to 1980: Elmo appears as a background Muppet; modern Elmo from November 18, 1985 (performed by Kevin Clash until 2012, Ryan Dillon since 2013).
- 1985: the adults finally meet Mr. Snuffleupagus (the Season 17 premiere): from then on "we'll believe you whenever you tell us something".
- 1991: Rosita; 1998: Elmo's World; 2005: Healthy Habits for Life and the "sometimes food" message; 2006: Abby Cadabby.
- 2015: moves to HBO; 2017: Julia debuts on TV; 2018: Cookie Monster's Foodie Truck; 2021: Tango, Wes and Elijah, Ji-Young introduced.
- 2025: Netflix and PBS KIDS; Season 56 reimagined with Tales from 123. 2026: Season 57 (November 23).
