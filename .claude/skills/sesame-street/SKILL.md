# Sesame Street (ss)

> **READ FIRST (2026-11-13): `references/license-guidelines.md` is the partner's own rulebook and overrides everything below and in the other references.** The short version: characters never do business (no endorsing, holding or touching the product, no product or brand name, no benefit claims, no CTA, nothing "in their voice" about the product; only catchphrases and generic true-to-character phrases), never flip Cookie Monster, the Count or Rosita, keep colors and scale, never crop pupils, one art style per project, clean uncluttered compositions, the 2D logo unaltered with clear space, no recreated Sesame Street set (no 123 street sign), no songs or voices, the legal notice "TM and © 2026 Sesame Workshop." on every marketing material, and **everything is vetted by Sesame Workshop before it is made and before it is posted**.

First Day has a partnership with Sesame Workshop (told by the owner on 2026-11-09). **No public announcement was found** on 2026-11-09 (two searches), so treat the partnership as confidential until the owner says otherwise: never write "official partner" or "approved by Sesame Workshop" in copy unless the owner confirms the exact wording and approval. The pages that use it will be explained by the owner case by case.

This skill is **material, not a template**: it teaches the world, the voices and the rules so that stories can be invented, not filled in. Read the references you need, they are short and indexed below.

## The Sesame Street way, in ten lines
1. **The mission is the filter.** Sesame Workshop: help children "grow smarter, stronger, and kinder". Every story teaches or models something small and real (name a feeling, try a new food, take turns). A funny page that teaches nothing is not Sesame.
2. **Warm, never scary, never shaming.** The child is never the problem, the parent is never the villain (this matches First Day's own rule). Mistakes are how characters learn (Big Bird, Grover, Abby).
3. **Each character has one clear want and one way of talking.** Cookie Monster wants cookies and says "Me". Elmo is 3 and a half and talks about himself as "Elmo". The Count counts. Oscar is grouchy about everything nice. Write the want first, the joke follows.
4. **Short, concrete, repeated.** Simple sentences, one idea each, repetition and call and response, questions asked straight to the reader ("Can you find...?").
5. **Two levels of humor.** The show is built for co-viewing: a silly layer for the child and a wink for the parent (parodies, wordplay, celebrity guests). Keep the child layer innocent and the parent layer gentle.
6. **Real fact, then play.** The show mixes fantasy and reality on purpose (research found children lose interest in street-only scenes). First Day pages do the same: a real fact or moment first, a character brings it to life.
7. **Food is a "sometimes / anytime" conversation, not a ban.** Cookie Monster's own message since 2005 is moderation. A vitamin gummy is a supplement, not a treat to chase. See the healthy eating reference before any food or gummy story.
8. **Feelings are a main subject now.** Seasons 55 to 57 are built on emotional well-being, kindness and big feelings (jealousy, sadness, being left out, calming down with a "heart breath" in Season 57). Bedtime, tantrum and picky-plate stories fit this well.
9. **Inclusive by design.** The cast is diverse on purpose (Rosita is bilingual, Julia is autistic, Gabrielle, Tamir, Ji-Young and others). Never reduce a character to a stereotype, and never invent a trait for them.
10. **Never invent canon.** If a fact is not in the references, do not state it. Flag it and ask. A wrong catchphrase or a fake storyline is worse than a plain page.

## Way of working for a Sesame page
1. State the single job of the page and the product (as in `firstday-copy`), then pick **one** character whose want matches the job (see the casting table in `references/characters.md`). One lead character, one supporting character at most.
2. Write the real moment and the real fact first (the plate pushed away, the 3 pm crash, the bedtime stall), in First Day's voice.
3. Let the character take the scene: their want, their way of talking, one signature bit. Keep it short.
4. Close with the First Day action in plain words (the character can point, the page asks).
5. Run the checks below. Log owner corrections in `firstday-copy/references/owner-feedback-log.md` (date, before, after, why).

## Checks before saying done
- Is every character fact in the references (or flagged as unverified)? No invented catchphrases, relationships or episodes.
- Does the character sound like themselves, in the same few ways? Is the line something a child could understand at once?
- Is there a real fact or moment, and is every product claim compliant (asterisk, "supports")? Did a character say anything First Day could not say?
- Is there anything scary, shaming, sarcastic at a child, or about weight, body size or "good/bad" kids? Remove it.
- Is the gummy shown as a supplement taken as directed, not as candy or a reward?
- Does it say or imply Sesame Workshop approval, endorsement or a medical effect? Only if the owner approved that exact wording.
- Does the art come only from the owner's library, in Version B (the owner's choice), never mixed, never redrawn or generated from words? Did any licensed art get committed to the public repo without the owner's approval?
- Copy rules: title case titles, no widows, no dashes, colons or semicolons in new copy, emoji only in chips.
